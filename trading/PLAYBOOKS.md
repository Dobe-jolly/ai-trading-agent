# Playbooks: build and test in phases

This file is written **before any playbook result exists**. Rules, parameters and the pass/fail gate
below are fixed in advance. Changing a rule after seeing its results is how noise gets mistaken for
edge, so it is not allowed: a playbook that fails is shelved, and a new variant needs a written reason
that doesn't use the results.

## How every playbook is tested (Phase 0, built)

`python -m trading playbooks NAME` replays the rule over history and reports per asset class.

- **Entry** at the open of the bar after the signal. One trade at a time per symbol.
- **Exit** at a fixed stop, a fixed target, or a time limit, all set by the rule and measured in ATRs at the signal bar.
- **Pessimistic fills:** if stop and target share a bar the stop comes first; gaps fill at the open; one full spread per trade.
- **Result in R** (1R = the initial stop).
- **Placebo:** random entries, random direction, same stop, target and time limit. It shows what the trade geometry and costs earn by themselves.
- **No lookahead:** every playbook must pass `lookahead_violations` (the rule gives the same answer on data cut at bar t).
- **Out of sample:** the last 40% of each symbol's history is scored separately.

**Gate (per asset class), all at once:** at least 100 trades; average R > 0; t >= 3; out of sample at least 40 trades,
average R > 0, t >= 1.65; and at least 0.05 R better than the placebo.

The machine itself was checked first: it passes a planted momentum edge, fails a random walk, fails a reversed rule,
and fails an edge that costs consume.

## Expectations, stated honestly

- The earlier signal study found nothing in sweeps, gaps, breaks or regime. Most retail rules fail an honest test; I expect most of these to.
  The aim of the phases is to close each question quickly and cheaply, not to guarantee a winner.
- The Deriv synthetic indices are generated by a random process. I expect Phases 2-3 on synthetics to come back null, and the test is there to confirm it.
- Each (playbook, asset class) pair is one test. With the gate above, the chance that a rule with no edge passes one test is well under 1%.
  If many pairs are tested, I will say how many.

## Phase 1: rules (fixed; parameters are conventional values, not fitted)

Both use the repo's own definitions, so what is tested is what the model sees: swings need 3 bars each side,
trend and range come from `geometry.classify_regime`, ATR is Wilder's 14-bar ATR.

### Alpha: structural pullback in a trend
- **Timeframe:** H4. **Markets:** forex and metals, tested separately.
- **Long** when all hold at the close of bar t: the regime is BULLISH_TREND (higher swing highs and higher swing lows);
  the bar's low came within 1.0 ATR of the most recent confirmed swing low; the close is above that swing low;
  and the bar closed up (close > open).
- **Short:** the mirror image.
- **Trade:** stop 1.5 ATR, target 3.0 ATR (2R), time limit 30 bars.
- Idea: buy a pullback to the last higher low in an uptrend once price shows it held.

### Beta: London-morning breakout of the Asian range
- **Timeframe:** H1. **Markets:** forex and metals, tested separately.
- **Asian range:** high and low of the H1 bars from 00:00 to 06:59 UTC that day. It must be between 0.5 and 2.5 ATR wide, or no trade that day.
- **Signal:** the first H1 bar closing beyond the range during 07:00 to 10:59 UTC: above the high is a buy, below the low is a sell.
  Only the first breakout of the day counts.
- **Trade:** stop 1.5 ATR, target 3.0 ATR, time limit 12 bars.
- Assumes the broker's candle times are UTC (true for Deriv's servers).
- Two implementation details, fixed here before any result: a day with fewer than 4 Asian-session bars (a data gap) is skipped,
  and Alpha reads the structure from the last 150 bars.
- Idea: the quiet Asian session builds a range, and London's liquidity pushes price out of it.

### What happens after Phase 1
- A playbook that **passes** in an asset class goes to Phase 4 for that class only.
- A playbook that **fails** is shelved as it stands. Its per-symbol breakdown is kept for the record but is not used to adjust it.
- A pass is a historical result, not a promise: it still has to survive the demo comparison.

Purpose: close the one near miss (Alpha on metals, +0.102 R, t 1.75) on data the rules have never seen.

- **Data:** XAUUSD H4 from the start of the terminal's history (Jan 2011) up to, but not including, 2021-10-26, the first bar of the first test.
  No bar overlaps the first test. Silver is not included because its H4 history from that period will not load.
- **Rule and gate:** Alpha exactly as above, the same gate, the same placebo seed. One run. No parameter changes.

## Phase 2: mean reversion, rules written 2026-10-27, approved and run 2026-10-28

### Designed around costs
Phase 1 measured the cost problem: the placebo loses **0.091 R per trade on H1 forex** (the spread), but only **0.010 R on H4 forex**,
because H4 candles are about twice as tall and the spread is the same. Mean reversion takes many small trades, so it is the style that
costs hurt most. The design therefore:
1. **Uses H4 only.** Cost drag per trade is about nine times lower than on H1.
2. **Uses a 2.0 ATR stop** (Phase 1 used 1.5), which cuts the spread's share of 1R by another quarter. At those sizes the spread costs
   roughly 0.01 to 0.03 R per forex trade and under 0.01 R on gold.
3. **Uses a symmetric trade, stop 2.0 ATR and target 2.0 ATR.** A random entry then earns about zero before costs, so a result above the
   placebo can only come from the entry's timing. (Mean reversion usually takes a small target with a big stop, which hides a low
   win rate behind a high one. Symmetric avoids that, and the break-even win rate is about 51%.)
4. **Keeps the same gate and placebo.** Nothing about the bar is lowered to suit the style.

Both rules use a **time limit of 12 bars** (two days on H4), after which the trade closes at the market. Same fills as Phase 1: entry at the next open,
stop first within a bar, gaps at the open, one spread per trade.

### Theta: fade a statistical extreme
- **Timeframe:** H4. **Markets:** forex, metals, and Volatility indices (see "Control" below), each tested separately.
- **z** at bar t = (close at t minus the average of the last 20 closes) divided by their standard deviation, the 20 closes including bar t.
- **Buy** when z falls to -2.0 or lower and the previous bar's z was above -2.0 (a fresh extreme, not a continuing one).
  **Sell** when z rises to +2.0 or higher and the previous bar's z was below +2.0.
- No other filter: this measures raw statistical reversion.

### Gamma: fade the edge of a range, only in a ranging market
- **Timeframe:** H4. **Markets:** forex, metals, Volatility indices (control), each tested separately.
- **Range:** the highest high and lowest low of the 20 bars **before** bar t (bar t is not included). Its width must be between 2.0 and 6.0 ATR.
- **Regime:** `geometry.classify_regime` must say RANGE (the swing structure is not a clean trend), read from the last 150 bars, as in Alpha.
- **Buy** when the bar's low came within 0.25 ATR of the range low, the close is still above the range low (no breakdown), and the bar closed up.
  **Sell** is the mirror image at the range high.
- Idea: in a market that is not trending, a touch of the edge that holds tends to bounce back toward the middle.

### Control: Volatility indices should fail
The Volatility indices are produced by a random process with constant volatility, so a mean-reversion rule has nothing to find there. They are tested
as a **negative control**: if either playbook PASSES on them, that is treated as a fault in the test machine, not a discovery, and nothing is acted on
until it is understood. Boom, Crash and Step are left for Phase 3.

### Procedure
- Data: 8,000 H4 bars per symbol (about 3.5 to 5 years), the symbols already in use: 9 forex pairs, XAUUSD and XAGUSD, and the 10 Volatility indices.
- One run per playbook, placebo seed 0, the gate above, no parameter changes after seeing a result.
- 6 tests in all (2 playbooks x forex, metals, Volatility indices). The Volatility cells are controls, so 4 count as real attempts.
- A **fail** shelves the playbook for that class as it stands. A **pass** in forex or metals goes forward to Phase 4 for that class only, and would
  first be re-tested once on earlier history it has not seen, as was done for Alpha on gold.

### Honest expectation
Slight mean reversion has been reported in some currency pairs over short horizons, but z-score fades are famous for failing in trends, and the
Phase 1 results show how little room costs leave. I expect most or all cells to fail. The aim is to close the question properly.

What the numbers say, without chasing them:
- **Forex:** both rules are about zero before costs (Theta equals its placebo exactly; Gamma is 0.011 R below). The H4 cost design worked: the placebo drag is only 0.005 to 0.021 R. The rules just carry no timing information.

Across six cells, one at t 1.7 on a random process is what chance produces, and it is not treated as a lead. Nothing is acted on.

## Phase 3: Boom/Crash and Step, rules written and approved 2026-10-28

Both are random processes by construction (Boom and Crash: a small steady drift plus memoryless random spikes; Step: a symmetric walk in fixed
steps), so the expected result is null. They are tested because the model's system prompt currently tells it that "after a large spike, price often
mean-reverts" and that "mean-reversion setups work well" on Step Index. Phase 3 checks whether those two statements hold up. Same machine, same gate,
same placebo, one run each, no tuning.

### Delta: fade a spike on Boom and Crash
- **Timeframe: M5, not H1.** Boom 1000 spikes about once per 33 minutes and Boom 300 about once per 10, so almost every H1 bar contains a spike and
  "a spike bar" would mean nothing. On M5 a spike bar is the exception. (This replaces the "H1" in the earlier outline, for that stated reason.)
- **Markets:** Boom 300, 500, 1000 and Crash 300, 500, 1000, scored together as one class (`boom_crash`).
- **Spike bar:** the bar's body (close minus open) is at least 3.0 times the ATR measured at the **previous** bar (a spike must not inflate the ATR it is
  judged against). Up spikes are Boom's and down spikes are Crash's, so the rule reads the bar, not the symbol name.
- **Signal:** at the close of a spike bar, trade **against** it: sell after an up spike, buy after a down spike (which is also the direction of the
  drift). Another spike bar within the trade is not special-cased.
- **Trade:** stop 2.0 ATR, target 2.0 ATR, time limit 12 bars (one hour). ATR is Wilder's 14-bar ATR at the signal bar, as in every playbook.
- **Known weakness of the test, stated now:** the machine fills a stop at its price whenever a bar's range reaches it. A real spike is a jump through the
  stop, so a real fill would be worse. This flatters short-Boom and long-Crash trades. A pass here would therefore not be trusted until fills were
  re-checked with slippage; a fail is unaffected.

### Epsilon: fade a statistical extreme on Step Index
- **Timeframe:** M15. **Market:** Step Index (the one symbol on the watchlist), class `step`.
- **Rule:** exactly Theta's: z of the close against the last 20 closes, buy on a fresh z of -2.0 or lower, sell on a fresh z of +2.0 or higher.
- **Trade:** stop 2.0 ATR, target 2.0 ATR, time limit 12 bars. Implemented as a subclass of Theta that changes only the market and timeframe, so the
  rule is the one already tested.
- Idea: Step moves in tiny equal steps with no memory, so a stretched price has no reason to snap back. This confirms or refutes the prompt's claim.

### Procedure
- Delta: 30,000 M5 bars per symbol (about 100 days), 6 symbols. Epsilon: 30,000 M15 bars (about 10 months), 1 symbol. Seed 0, one run each.
- 2 tests in all. Both are expected nulls, so neither counts as a hope; a PASS is treated first as a fault in the machine or the data, as with the Volatility control.
- A fail shelves the rule. Whatever the outcome, if a prompt statement is refuted I will propose changing that prompt text (not do it unasked).

## Phase 3 results (run once on 2026-10-28, rules and gate as approved above, placebo seed 0)

Delta on M5 (Boom and Crash 300, 500, 1000; 30,000 bars each, 2026-07-15 to 2026-10-28). Epsilon on M15 (Step Index, 30,000 bars, 2025-12-19 to 2026-10-28).

| Playbook | Class | Trades | Win% | Avg R | t | Out of sample R (t) | Placebo R | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delta | boom_crash | 4,792 | 57.1 | +0.067 | +4.92 | +0.064 (+2.94) | -0.084 | **PASS** |
| Epsilon | step | 1,357 | 48.8 | -0.023 | -0.96 | -0.003 (-0.08) | -0.017 | FAIL, as expected |

Epsilon is shelved: the prompt's claim that mean reversion works on Step Index is not supported (it is about zero before costs).

**Delta passed the gate. That is not accepted as a finding.** Phase 3 said a pass would be treated first as a fault in the machine or data, and two
faults are plausible on a Boom/Crash index, which has a steady drift plus memoryless random spikes:
1. **Fills.** The machine ends a stopped trade at exactly -1R (the stop price). A real spike is a jump through the stop, so a short on Boom that meets a
   spike loses several R, not one. Delta trades against the spike, i.e. with the drift, so it is the rule most flattered by this.
2. **Drift, not spikes.** The gate's placebo trades random directions, so the drift cancels in it. A rule that simply always trades with the drift
   (sell Boom, buy Crash) beats it without the spike condition adding anything.

### Registered before running: two diagnostic checks on Delta (one run each, no parameter changes)
- **Jump stops.** If a bar touches the stop and also CLOSES beyond it, the fill is the bar's close (worse than the stop); otherwise the stop price.
  Applied to the real rule and the placebo alike. Reading: Delta must still pass the full gate under this rule, or the pass was a fill artifact.
- **Drift placebo.** Random entries, but always in the drift direction (sell on Boom, buy on Crash, read from the symbol name), same stop, target,
  time limit and entry rate as Delta, under the jump-stop fills. Reading: the spike condition has value only if Delta beats this by at least 0.05 R in
  average R, in total and out of sample. If it does not, Delta is "trade with the drift",
  which is a known property of the index, not a spike rule, and the system prompt's "spike fade" claim is unsupported.
- Both checks are descriptive and cannot rescue a failure. Delta is a candidate for Phase 4 only if it clears both.

### Result of the two checks (run once, 2026-10-28; same six symbols, 30,000 M5 bars, jump-stop fills for every row)

| Row | Trades | Win% | Avg R (t) | Out of sample R (t) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delta | 4,792 | 57.2 | -0.095 (-5.49) | -0.105 (-3.78) |
| Random-direction placebo | 5,033 | 50.4 | -0.218 (-13.20) | -0.218 (-8.34) |
| Drift placebo (sell Boom, buy Crash) | 5,011 | 58.9 | -0.110 (-6.20) | -0.103 (-3.61) |

- **Check 1, jump stops: FAIL.** Under realistic fills Delta averages -0.095 R (t -5.49), not +0.067 R. The earlier pass was a fill artifact: the machine had
  capped every spike loss at -1R.
- **Check 2, drift placebo: FAIL.** Delta beats trading blindly with the drift by only +0.015 R in total and -0.002 R out of sample (needed +0.05 in both).
  The spike condition adds nothing: Delta is "trade with the drift", which loses money here after costs. Its 57% win rate is the drift's win rate (59% for the placebo).
- Per symbol, Delta is negative on five of the six (Crash 300 is +0.054 on 298 trades, which at that size is noise).

**Delta is shelved. Phase 3 is closed with no survivors.** The gate's random-direction placebo could not see either problem: a lesson for any future rule on
a market with jumps or drift is to run jump-stop fills and a drift-direction placebo as part of the first test, not after a pass.

**Consequence for the system prompt (not changed, awaiting your decision):** two statements are unsupported by these tests.
"Spike fade: after a large spike, price often mean-reverts" (Boom/Crash) and "Mean-reversion and range-compression setups work well" (Step Index).
